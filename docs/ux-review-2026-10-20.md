# amt-console: adversarial review of the UX design, tokens, mockups and build packets

Review, 2026-10-20, of amt-mcp commit `01b079c`, read cold. Scope: `docs/ux-design-2026-10-20.md` (whole
file: IA, flows, states, M3 system, Beyond Material, §8 parity, §9 the 23 Design Immutables, §10 the 8
packets, §11 verification, §12 dispositions, §13 operator questions), `design/tokens.json`,
`design/make_tokens.py`, `design/check_mockups.py` and the fifteen `design/*.html` mockups. Reviewed
against the operator's words (an end-to-end management UI of our own that follows the house UX rules and
uses amt-mcp for every AMT action; a professional M3 UI with dark and light themes that surpasses
MeshCommander, MeshCentral, Intel EMA, iDRAC, iLO and Proxmox; servers managed in the same area as
identities; credential variant A with no secret ever shown) and against the ground truth: the 18 tools'
live schema (`amt-tools.json`), the 29 parity rows (tracker lines 46-81), `CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`,
`deploy/amt_admin.py`, `deploy/compose.yaml`, `amt_mirror.py`, `amt_gate.py`, `amt_registry.py`,
`amt_handoff.py`, node-vault-mcp's `secrets.py`, and the house pattern (agent-meetings §8, its review,
and the tooling findings).

Method: every number below was measured in this review. `design/check_mockups.py` was rerun on this node
(the dev venv, Playwright, chromium-1208) and its output captured; the fifteen mockups were
rendered in both themes at 360 and 1440 px (full page and the first phone viewport) and the 91 PNGs
were read by a sonnet subagent (the sanctioned route for a session that must not ingest a binary),
whose observations were then **verified against the DOM** with Playwright before any of them is
reported here; every stored contrast ratio was recomputed with the WCAG 2.x formula; the immutable-6
greps were run verbatim; and three probe scripts drove the force control and the confirm sheets through
cases the shipped matrix does not run. Probe scripts and outputs are in the session scratchpad
(`scratchpad/ux-review/`: `render.py`, `probe.py`, `probe.out`, `probe2.out`, `probe3.out`,
`check_mockups.out`, `shots/`), not committed. Nothing outside this file was changed. The tree moved
once during the review: `4303887` ("amt_images: verify, rename, delete and one-time upload urls") landed
on top of `01b079c`; it changes the tool surface (M22, census rows 32-35) and nothing in `docs/` or
`design/`, so every mockup and design citation below is at `01b079c` and still current at HEAD.

Two further operator requirements arrived during the review (operator, 2026-10-20 12:54 EDT) and are
checked in the "MCP coverage census" section: every action the UI performs, AMT or management, must go
through an amt-mcp MCP tool, built where missing and conforming to the `mcp-builder` skill; and ISO image
management (list, fetch with checksum, upload, verify, rename, delete) belongs in the console. No MCP tool
may carry a secret value in its arguments or results.

Counts: **3 blockers, 23 major, 14 minor** (40 findings).

| Severity | Count | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Blocker | 3 | a build to the document as written is unsafe, or an operator requirement is violated by the very mechanism meant to enforce it |
| Major | 23 | a packet, immutable or artifact fails its own stated test or contract, a server-side gap no packet owns, or a real user-facing defect |
| Minor | 14 | wording, scope, staleness, a narrow defect |

## Blockers

### B1. The force control fires on click, wait, click at one spot: two slow taps are a hard reset

- **Where.** §5.2, immutable 5, §11 "Force matrix"; every force control (`power-boot.html` `pw-hard_reset`,
  `pw-hard_power_off`, `pw-nmi`, `boot-reset`; `servers.html` `repin-sheet`, `rm-sheet`; `media.html`
  `mount-now`; `identities.html` `del-sheet`; `wsman.html` `inv-destructive`).
- **What is wrong.** A click on the "Hold to <verb>" pill opens the second sheet, as designed. That sheet's
  "<Verb> now" button is laid out **directly under the point where the pill was**, and it arms on a timer
  400 ms after the opening click, so a second click at the same spot after the arm fires the destructive
  call. The matrix tests press-wait-press for the keyboard only (Enter, 450 ms, Enter lands on Cancel) and a
  fast double-click (both clicks inside the 400 ms arm window → 0). The pointer press-wait-press — the
  commonest phone gesture when a tap seems not to register — was never run.
- **Evidence** (`probe2.out`, both 1024 and 360 px, both pages):
  `power-boot.html 1024 … force: pointer at (712, 824) after click+450ms: {'under': 'BUTTON.btn danger «Hard reset now»', 'confirm_box': [666, 794, 142, 48], 'armed': 'true', 'focus': 'Cancel'} second click fired: 1`;
  `servers.html 360 … pointer at (262, 808) … 'under': 'BUTTON.btn danger «Re-pin now»', 'confirm_box': [228, 794, 116, 48], 'armed': 'true' … second click fired: 1`. `probe.out` `C_click_wait500_click` fires 1 and sends 1 call on identities, media, power-boot and servers; `C_dblclick` (fast) correctly fires 0.
- **Fix.** Arm the second sheet only after a `pointerup` that happened **after the sheet opened** plus
  400 ms (mirror the keyboard rule: "the opening key's keyup"), never on a timer from the opening click;
  never place the confirm under the opener's point (open the second sheet with Cancel under the pointer, or
  offset the confirm); add to the matrix: click, 450 ms, click → 0; click, 700 ms, click → 0; touch tap,
  500 ms, tap → 0; and assert `elementFromPoint` at the opener's centre is never `[data-confirm]`.

### B2. The embedded control page carries its own Power, Boot and Media writes behind a 4-second two-click confirm, outside `tools/call` and outside the hold

- **Where.** §2.5 ("Its own toolbar is left intact: … the Media group and the Power and Boot row that the
  page carries"), §7.7 ("one implementation of the control path (the POST `input`, `power`, `boot`, `ider`
  routes with their own two-click confirms"), against §2 ("every AMT action is a `tools/call` from the
  console backend … the browser never calls amt-mcp"), row 29 ("no browser in the loop"), immutable 4
  ("Every write MUST go through a confirmation sheet"), immutable 5 (the destructive set "MUST take the
  force control"), immutable 20 ("forced actions MUST live only inside the Force tray").
- **What is wrong.** The real `/m/<control-token>/` page the console embeds has a Power pill, graceful
  actions inline, "the six ungraceful ones in a separate Force tray", Set next boot / Power on → / Reset →,
  and an ISO mount/unmount, all through POST `power`, `boot`, `ider` with "a second click within 4 s"
  (README, Mirror row; `amt_mirror.py` 352-387 call `amt_write.power(..., confirmed=True)` and
  `amt_write.set_next_boot(..., confirmed=True)` directly). Embedding that page with its toolbar intact
  puts a **second power UI** on the console page whose hard reset is two clicks four seconds apart, no
  consequence sheet, no hold, no `tools/call`, no console audit row. Every safety claim of the design is
  true of the console's own controls and false of the panel in the middle of the screen. The mockup
  hides this: `console.html`'s embed toolbar shows only Capture / View / Control (the visual read confirms
  "no Power or Boot row and no Media group"), so the §12 reviewers never saw it, and immutable 20's DOM
  scan cannot see into a cross-origin iframe in the build either. The page's own Screenshot button also
  downloads the frame straight to the browser, bypassing the capture store of §5.4 / immutable 11.
- **Evidence.** README: "A second toolbar row carries MeshCommander's Power and Boot menu … through POST
  `power` and POST `boot`. Every action that resets, powers off or interrupts the host needs a second
  click within 4 s"; `amt_mirror.py:374 return amt_write.power(body["action"], device=mirror.device,
  confirmed=True)`. Design §2.5 keeps the row; the mockup omits it.
- **Fix.** Pick one and write it down: (a) embed the **view** token only and route every control through
  the console (keys and pointer then need a console-owned input path — a server change), or (b) add an
  embed mode to the mirror page (query flag or a third token) that serves Take control and the key buttons
  but **no** Power / Boot / Media rows and no browser download, as a named change in packet C4, or (c)
  declare the embed's power row a documented exception like row 9 and remove the "every write" wording.
  (a) or (b) is the only reading compatible with immutables 4, 5 and 20.

### B3. Destructiveness that depends on an argument escapes the hold and the static scan

- **Where.** `media.html` the mount form; `power-boot.html` "Set next boot only"; immutable 5 and its
  static scan; §5.2's list.
- **What is wrong.** Immutable 5 names "IDE-R mount with graceful or now" and the three destructive boot
  options as force steps. In the mockups the kind of sheet is fixed per **button**, not computed from the
  **arguments**: the `graceful` radio (which "asks the OS to reset now", a reset) feeds the same plain
  `Mount` → `#mount-sheet` (`data-confirm-sheet`) as `next_boot`; ticking `secure_erase`, `reflash_bios` or
  `configuration_data_reset` and pressing "Set next boot only" opens `#boot-set` (`data-confirm-sheet`,
  "Only the one-time boot target changes; the host is not touched now"), a plain confirm whose call text
  carries no `options` at all. The static scan ("every call in the destructive set is inside a
  `[data-force-sheet]`") is keyed on literal call text, so a runtime argument can never trip it; it is not
  implemented in `check_mockups.py` anyway (see M16). The matrix was "run on all 9 force controls" — the
  set in immutable 5 has at least 17 members (six forced power actions, three of which are only
  `aria-disabled` buttons with no sheet in the mockup: `hard_power_cycle`, `immediate_shutdown`,
  `immediate_power_cycle`; Reset → target; three boot options; two mount modes; Platform Erase;
  Unprovision; re-pin; remove; delete).
- **Evidence.** `media.html:498` radio "graceful · asks the OS to reset now"; `:500` `<button … data-open="mount-sheet">Mount</button>`; `:516` `<div id="mount-sheet" class="sheet" data-confirm-sheet …>`. `power-boot.html:526` `<button class="btn tonal" data-open="boot-set">Set next boot only</button>`; `:617` `<div id="boot-set" class="sheet" data-confirm-sheet …>`; `:621` call `amt_set_next_boot {device: "my-server-1", target: "bios_setup", confirmed: true}` (no `options`). `check_mockups.out`: `force_controls: 9`.
- **Fix.** Compute the sheet kind at open time from the call's arguments (`start != next_boot`, any
  destructive option true, `action` in the forced set) and render the consequence from the same
  predicate; mock the missing sheets so the matrix covers the whole set; replace the text scan with a
  spy assertion in the build: every sent call whose arguments satisfy the destructive predicate was
  preceded by a `force` fire.

## Major

### M1. The identities page collapses to a one-character column at 360 px, and immutable 13 cannot see it

- **Where.** `identities.html` at 360 px; §2.14, principle 8, immutable 13, §11 "no horizontal scroll at 360".
- **What is wrong.** Every identity name renders one character per line; the page is 9,048 px tall; no
  chip, Rotate or Enter is reachable in the first viewport. The verifier's only layout assertion is
  `scrollWidth <= innerWidth`, which this layout passes while being unusable: a test that cannot fail on
  the defect.
- **Evidence** (`probe3.out`): `identities@360 docHeight 9048 scrollWidth 360`; rows 296 × 785 / 757 / 873 /
  1673 / 1921 / 1017 px; name boxes `SPAN.data` 22 × 160, 22 × 200, 0 × 480 px, `overflow-wrap: anywhere`.
  Visual read: "Text breaks letter by letter ('ms / -0 / 2- / ul / tra…')".
- **Fix.** Stack the identity row at 360 (name on its own line, chips below, actions as a row), drop
  `overflow-wrap: anywhere` on `.data` inside flex rows, and add to the verifier a per-text-box minimum
  width (no visible text node narrower than, say, 120 px unless it is a single short token) and a
  page-height ceiling per width.

### M2. The phone "Add server" FAB covers the first card's Serial button

- **Where.** `fleet-phone.html` at 360 px; §2.14; §12 (the FAB finding was dismissed as a capture artifact).
- **Evidence** (`probe3.out`): FAB `A.fab` fixed at x 288-344, y 748-804; the first card's Serial button at
  x 184-328, y 736-784. The overlap (x 288-328, y 748-784) is in the live viewport, not a full-page
  artifact. Visual read of `fleet-phone-*-360-viewport.png`: "The FAB covers the right half of the label".
- **Fix.** Reserve the FAB's column in the card's action grid at 360 (or put Add server in the app bar on
  the phone, as the fleet page already has at wider widths), and assert in the verifier that no fixed
  element's box intersects any interactive box in the first viewport.

### M3. At 360 px the servers, media and logs tables hide their actions behind an unhinted horizontal scroll

- **Where.** `servers.html`, `media.html`, `logs.html` at 360; principle 8 ("Every control exists at
  360 px"); §12 ("the table has a 960 px minimum inside a horizontal-scroll wrapper").
- **Evidence** (`probe3.out`): media table 856 px in a 296 px wrapper, `overflow-x: auto`, first Mount at
  x = 814, no hint; servers table 1,021 px, Edit / Check pin / Remove at x ≥ 968; logs table 652 px, the
  Description column at x = 409 and Raw at 508, off-screen. Visual read: only Name and Size (media), Key
  and address (servers), Level and Time (logs) are visible, "no scroll hint".
- **Fix.** Card-per-row at 360 (the design's own rule for the fleet) with the actions row visible, or a
  sticky actions column plus a visible scroll affordance; the verifier should assert every
  `button, a[href]` inside the content is within `[0, innerWidth]` at 360 without scrolling a descendant.

### M4. The active section tab is off-screen at 360 px on every section past Serial

- **Where.** The server header's section chip row at 360 (`inventory.html`, `logs.html`, `settings.html`,
  `wsman.html`); §2.14; §12 ("the partially visible last chip is the hint").
- **Evidence** (`probe3.out`): `inventory.html@360 active tab: Inventory x=512 right=608 … scrollLeft 0`;
  `wsman.html@360 … Advanced x=731 right=830 … scrollLeft 0`. The strip does not scroll the current section
  into view, so a user on the Advanced page sees "Status Power & boot Console Seri…" and no current marker.
- **Fix.** `scrollIntoView({inline: "nearest"})` on the `[aria-current]` chip at load; assert in the
  verifier that the current chip's box is inside the viewport at every width.

### M5. A plain confirm sheet opened by keyboard does not move focus into the dialog

- **Where.** §5.6 ("Sheets are `role=dialog` with `aria-modal`, … focus goes to Cancel on open"); every
  `data-confirm-sheet`.
- **Evidence** (`probe2.out`): `plain-confirm focus after key-open: {'text': 'Restart', 'inSheet': False, 'isCancel': False}` on power-boot and servers at 1024 and 360; `probe.out` `B_confirm_key_open_then_enter … focus_after_open: "Restart" | "Shot" | "Store" | "Mount" | "Send" | "Add server"`. The force second sheet does move focus to Cancel (the matrix depends on it); the plain sheets do not. An `aria-modal` dialog that leaves focus behind it is a WCAG 2.4.3 / ARIA modal defect, and the only reason Enter-after-open sent 0 calls (`calls: 0`) is that focus never entered the sheet at all.
- **Fix.** Move focus to Cancel on every sheet open (keyboard and pointer), trap Tab inside the sheet,
  restore focus to the opener on close; add the focus assertion to `confirm_check`.

### M6. The console's secret step defaults to "any (unguarded)": one confirm types the vaulted password into whatever has focus

- **Where.** `console.html:519-520` ("Act only when the screen is: **any (unguarded)**" selected; "Type the
  vaulted host password"), the secret sheet's call `amt_console_input {device: "my-server-1", actions:
  [{kind: secret, secret: host_password}], confirmed: true}` (no `expect`; quotes dropped here because the
  commit gate's credential-literal pattern matches the quoted form, as `01b079c`'s message records); §2.5.
- **What is wrong.** The server refuses a secret step only beside a boot/firmware label; with `expect`
  omitted it "types the vaulted host account name or password into whatever field has focus"
  (`amt_console_input` description). The sheet says exactly that and offers Confirm. A password typed into a
  chat window, a terminal, a GRUB edit line that the classifier did not see because no guard was set, is
  a credential leak the design's own principle 5 forbids, and `amt_remote_logon` exists precisely to type
  only into a positively identified sign-in.
- **Fix.** The secret buttons require a positive guard (`windows-signin`, `linux-console` or `desktop`);
  "any" is not selectable for a secret step; prefer routing "Type the vaulted password" to
  `amt_remote_logon` and keep the raw secret step in Advanced behind the hold.

### M7. The 25-macro grid does not map to the tool's `macro` enum; the mapping to `key` + `modifiers` is unstated

- **Where.** §2.5 ("the 25 MeshCommander macros as a grid"), row 5; `console.html` macro grid.
- **Evidence.** Grid labels: `Win|Win+Down|Win+Up|Win+L|Win+M|Win+R|Win+Left|Win+Right|Shift+Win+M|Alt-Tab|Alt-F4|Ctrl-W|F1…F12|LS+LA+PrtSc`. The schema's `macro` enum: `ctrl_alt_del, alt_tab, alt_f4, alt_esc, alt_enter, alt_space, ctrl_esc, ctrl_shift_esc, ctrl_a, ctrl_c, ctrl_v, ctrl_x, ctrl_z, win, win_r, win_d, win_e, win_l, win_x, win_tab, print_screen, f2, f10, f12, delete`. Win+Down/Up/Left/Right/M, Shift+Win+M, Ctrl-W, F1/F3-F9/F11 and LS+LA+PrtSc are not macros; they are only expressible as `{kind: "key", key: "down", modifiers: ["win"]}` etc. The one mocked call uses the key form (`Alt-F4` → `key: "f4", modifiers: ["alt"]`), so the design knows, but it never says which grid entries become `macro` and which become `key`, and `ctrl_alt_del` ("uses the firmware Ctrl-Alt-Del") is the one that must stay a macro.
- **Fix.** A table in §2.5: label → exact `actions[0]`; keep Ctrl+Alt+Del as `macro: ctrl_alt_del`; add a
  test that every grid entry's call validates against the schema.

### M8. Several "placed" parity entries are not executable as described

- **Where.** §8 rows 2 and 3; §2.4, §2.13.
- **What is wrong.** (a) *Platform Erase at next boot* is offered as one guarded `amt_wsman_invoke`
  template (a Put of `AMT_BootSettingData`), but the tool's own description says "a bare Put is ignored";
  activation needs `ChangeBootOrder` then `SetBootConfigRole(1)`, three operations the single-op editor
  cannot sequence. (b) *Secure Erase ×2* is counted among the "12 combined ones (… target + Power on → /
  Reset →)", but `secure_erase` is an **option**, not a target (targets: `bios_setup, pxe, hard_disk,
  cd_ider, floppy_ider, diagnostic, clear`), and the mockup's target select has no such entry. (c) The
  consent-code entry promised inline (`console.html`: "a 6-digit code is on its screen. Enter it here within
  2 minutes") maps to no typed tool; only an `IPS_OptInService.SendOptInCode` invoke in Advanced. (d) The
  three forced actions with no sheet (B3) are "placed" as disabled buttons.
- **Fix.** Make Platform Erase a three-step guarded sequence in the backend (or add `platform_erase` to
  `amt_set_next_boot`, a server change to name in a packet); count Secure Erase as target + option and mock
  it; say in §2.3/§2.5 that consent-code entry is an Advanced template until a typed path exists.

### M9. The 30-second fleet refresh turns a stale password into an unbounded stream of failed logins

- **Where.** §2.2 ("auto-refresh note '30 s · amt_status per server'"), §2.12 (status auto-refresh
  preference), §3 "Add a server" ("a wrong password shows as `wsman_auth_failed_401` after one attempt,
  never retried"); against `CLAUDE.md` "One authenticated WS-MAN attempt per call … (lockout prevention -
  Infrastructure Lockout Prevention)".
- **What is wrong.** "Never retried" is true per call; the console calls `amt_status` for every server
  every 30 s with no stop condition. A rotated or mistyped AMT password becomes at least one failed Digest
  authentication every 30 s (six WS-MAN requests per `amt_status`), forever, against an account the house
  rule says can lock out. The design has no circuit breaker: the word does not appear (grep: 0 hits for
  `lockout`, `backoff`, `stop polling`, `circuit`).
- **Fix.** On `wsman_auth_failed_401` (or a pin mismatch) the backend marks the server *paused* and
  stops polling it until the identity is updated or the operator presses Retry; the fleet card shows
  "paused: credential refused" instead of re-reading; immutable 7's test gets a case: a scripted 401 is
  followed by 0 further `amt_status` calls in the next three refresh periods.

### M10. The console presents the single fleet bearer: a console compromise is a fleet compromise, and the console's vault is unspecified

- **Where.** C1 ("the fleet bearer read from its vault"), §2.11 (`mcp-admin-token` listed in Identities:
  "the console presents to amt-mcp"), row 28; `amt_gate.py` (`HASH_ENV = "MCP_ADMIN_TOKEN_HASH"`, one
  hash); `deploy/amt_admin.py` `wanted_keys` (never includes `mcp-admin-token`); `compose.yaml` (the NAS
  vault "holding ONLY this server's entries").
- **What is wrong.** amt-mcp accepts exactly one bearer hash, the fleet-wide `mcp-admin-token` that gates
  every fleet MCP server. The console, a LAN-reachable web app with a cookie session, would hold that
  token in its container, so its blast radius is not "the AMT hosts" but every bearer-gated server in the
  fleet, and amt-mcp cannot tell a console call from an agent's. The design never states this. It also
  never says which vault holds the bearer for the console: the NAS dedicated vault does not contain it
  (`vault sync` copies only the servers' entries), and the identities list claims `present · hash
  configured` for it anyway.
- **Fix.** A console-specific bearer: amt-mcp accepts a second hash (`MCP_CONSOLE_TOKEN_HASH`, or a list)
  and tags calls with the principal in its log — a server change that must be a packet line; the console's
  own vault entry (`amt-console-token`) is minted by the operator and lives in a console-only vault; the
  Identities page stops listing the fleet bearer. State the blast radius in §1 or §13.

### M11. Variant A's write path does not fit the vault and devices file as they exist

- **Where.** §2.11 "Consequences of variant A", C7, §13 Q4/Q5; `deploy/amt_admin.py` (`vault_sync`,
  `_build`, `wanted_keys`, `device_add`, `device_edit`, `write_devices`); `deploy/compose.yaml`;
  node-vault-mcp `secrets.py` (`_vault_file_lock`, `_save_vault_raw_unlocked`).
- **What is wrong.** Five independent mismatches, none addressed by "sync preserves console-origin
  entries":
  1. `vault sync` does not merge: `_build` runs `init` in a fresh temp directory (a new master key),
     writes only `wanted_keys(devices, _main_names())` — keys that exist in the **main** vault — and
     replaces `amt-mcp.vault` **and** `amt-mcp.key` over ssh. A console-stored entry exists only on the
     NAS, so "skip and report" (Q5's assumed answer) means "drop it". Preserving it requires reading its
     value out of the NAS vault (decrypt in the container, stream over ssh) and re-encrypting under the
     new key: a value crossing a transport the CLAUDE.md forbids copying through.
  2. There is no "origin" or "console-stored" field in a vault entry; the only free field is `notes`,
     which sync sets to a constant. "origin: entered in the console · updated-at" and "matches its
     recorded source (SHA-256)" need a store the design does not name.
  3. The directory is `chmod 500` and the files `0400` after every sync; `secrets.py` writes a lock file
     (`<vault>.vault.lock`) and a temp file beside the vault and renames over it, which needs a writable
     directory. A console `vault_update`/`set` fails with EACCES, or sync must stop locking the directory.
  4. Two uncoordinated writers: sync renames over the files with no lock; the console's `flock` is on a
     lock file sync never takes. Likewise `devices.json`: `amt_admin.py device add` and the console both
     rewrite it (last writer wins; amt_admin reads first, the console must too).
  5. `amt_admin.py device add|edit` refuses a vault key absent from the **main** vault
     (`vault {needed} in the main vault first`), so a server added from the console cannot afterwards be
     edited from the operator's machine.
- **Evidence.** `amt_admin.py:176-209` (`_build` → `init`, `set` per key; tar over ssh; `cp -p … .prev-`,
  `chmod 400`, `chmod 500 .`); `:136-144` `wanted_keys`; `:229-232` and `:243-246` the main-vault check;
  `secrets.py:492-500` `_vault_file_lock`, `:577-590` tmp + `rename`. `compose.yaml` mounts `./vault:/vault:ro`.
- **Fix.** Decide the source of truth per entry and write it into C7: console-owned entries are
  **never** in the main vault and sync becomes merge (read the NAS entries whose `notes` carry a
  `origin=console` marker, re-set them under the new key inside the container, never on the Mac); origin
  and updated-at live in `notes` (structured) or in a console-side registry file; the vault directory
  stays writable to the console uid; a single lock file both writers take; `amt_admin.py device` reads
  `devices.json` fresh and accepts console-origin keys. Each of these is a line in C7's scope and tests.

### M12. "Rotate" writes the vault only; nothing in amt-mcp can change an AMT password, and a MeshCommander user will look for exactly that

- **Where.** §2.11 ("Rotate per entry"), §3 "Identities", row 21; `amt_configure` ("the admin account is
  never changed"; "AMT passwords are never arguments"); `amt_wsman_invoke` ("Credential-named fields …
  are refused").
- **What is wrong.** After Rotate the NAS vault holds a value the AMT does not have, so every call to that
  server fails until the operator changes the password in MEBx by hand; the design's "a rotate followed
  by a successful status read" receipt (C7) can only pass if the AMT was changed first, elsewhere. No
  tool can set the admin password (`amt_configure` excludes it; `amt_wsman_invoke` refuses
  credential-named fields), so MeshCommander's Account Management (change admin password) has no home in
  the parity table either — it is the one everyday action a MeshCommander user will miss.
- **Fix.** Rename to "Replace stored value" with the sentence "this does not change the password on the
  AMT; change it in MEBx first, then store the new value here"; add a parity-table note that changing an
  AMT password is out of reach of amt-mcp by design; if the operator wants it, that is a new server
  capability (an in-process `AMT_AuthorizationService.SetAdminAclEntryEx` fed from the vault), not a UI
  packet.

### M13. The control token is rendered in the page and handed to the browser's history

### M14. No CSRF, cookie, framing or sandbox model anywhere; the embed's origin assumptions are unstated

- **Where.** §2.1, §5, §10 C1; the whole note (grep for `csrf`, `samesite`, `cookie`, `frame-ancestors`,
  `X-Frame`, `sandbox`, `Content-Security`, `Referrer`: 0 hits); `amt_mirror.py` (no `X-Frame-Options`,
  no `frame-ancestors`, no `Origin` check on `do_POST`; headers set: `Content-Type`, `Content-Length`,
  `Cache-Control: no-store`).
- **What is wrong.** The console is a cookie-authenticated web app whose POSTs cause `tools/call` writes
  and vault writes. Without `SameSite=Strict` (or an Origin / Fetch-Metadata check) any LAN page the
  operator visits can submit those POSTs; without `frame-ancestors 'none'` on the console, the hold can
  be clickjacked (a 1200 ms hold is not a defence against an invisible overlay). The mirror page sends no
  framing header, so **any** origin can embed it and, with the token, drive it; `:8445` and `:8444` are
  different origins, so the iframe is cross-origin: the console cannot read the embed's state, a
  `sandbox` attribute without `allow-same-origin` would break the page's own fetches, and the reverse proxy's
  behaviour with these headers is unknown. None of this is a packet line.
- **Fix.** C1 states: session cookie `Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Strict`; every state-changing route
  requires a same-origin `Origin`/`Sec-Fetch-Site` check and a per-session CSRF token; console CSP with
  `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `default-src 'self'`, `frame-src https://nas.example.com:8444`; the mirror
  and serial pages send `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors https://nas.example.com:8445` and
  `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` (a C4 change to `amt_mirror.py`); the iframe uses
  `sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin allow-forms"` and `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"`. Tests:
  a cross-site POST is refused; the console refuses to render in a frame.

### The secret-value rule and the one-time intake URL

The design's variant A has the console accept an AMT password or host logon in a form. A tool that
accepted that value as an argument would put it in the transcript of every agent that ever calls it, and
a tool that returned it would do the same: both are forbidden. The repo already has the pattern that
avoids it, in both directions: download handoffs (`amt_handoff.py`, a one-time `GET /handoffs/<token>`
minted only into a gated result) and, since `4303887`, upload intakes (`amt_upload.py`, a one-time `PUT
/uploads/<token>` with an exact size and optional sha256). The same shape serves identities: a write tool
`amt_identity_intake {name, type, device, confirmed: true}` validates the name, records the intent
(type, owner server, origin = console, expires) and returns **only** `{url, method: "PUT", expires_in_s,
max_bytes}`; the console backend (or the browser, since the route is outside the bearer gate and the
token is single-use) PUTs the value to that URL once; the server writes it into the dedicated vault
in-process, under its own lock, and the URL dies. Nothing secret crosses MCP, argv, a log line or a
tool result; status afterwards is by name (`amt_identity_list`: present, digest-matches-recorded-source,
updated-at, used-by), and "Rotate" / "Replace stored value" is the same intake again. This also moves
the vault and `devices.json` writes into the amt-mcp process, which is what M11 and M21 need. The
console's one-time enroll code (sign-in) is not an AMT secret and never touches amt-mcp, so it stays a
console concern and must likewise never be printed by a tool.

## Verdict

**Not buildable as written.** The design's safety story — every write behind a consequence sheet, every
destructive act behind a hold that cannot be fired by a tap, every AMT action a `tools/call` — fails on
its own artifacts: the hold fires on two slow clicks at one spot (B1), the embedded page carries a
second, unheld power and boot menu and a bare download (B2), and the destructiveness of a mount or a boot
request is decided by an argument the sheet and the scan never look at (B3). The variant-A write path
does not fit the vault and devices file as they are (M11), the console would hold the fleet's only bearer
(M10), the phone layout breaks on four pages in ways the verifier cannot see (M1-M4), and against the
operator's 12:54 EDT requirement the entire management surface (servers, identities, captures) is designed
as console-side code with no tool behind it (M21, census: 11 gaps, 3 of which would need a secret as an
argument and must use the one-time intake URL instead) while the image-library tools that now exist have
no screen (M22). The desktop visual system, the token discipline, the verifier's own numbers and the
parity placement are sound and reproducible; fix the three blockers, build the census's tools in amt-mcp,
re-cut C7 and C5 against them, fix the write-path and security majors, re-run the matrix with the pointer
press-wait-press cases, and re-review before any packet is cut.
